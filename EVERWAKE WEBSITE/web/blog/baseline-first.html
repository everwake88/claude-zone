Baseline first: measure before you build

Most AI projects stall in the same place. A demo impresses, then nobody can say whether the system in production is actually better than what it replaced.

The question nobody asked in week one

"Is it working?" is impossible to answer three months after launch if nobody wrote down what "working" meant before it started. Confirmation time, recovery rate, conversion, average order value, repeat purchase — these are knowable numbers, but only if someone measures them before anything changes.

What we measure

Week one of every engagement baselines the numbers the project will be judged on, against the client's own data rather than an industry average:

  • How long an order waits before it is confirmed
  • How many abandoned carts are recovered today
  • What share of conversations end in an order
  • What a typical order is worth
  • How many customers come back

Why your numbers, not the industry's

An industry average tells you what is true somewhere else. A restaurant in Doha and a retailer in Cairo do not share a conversion rate, and a target borrowed from a slide deck is a target nobody believes. Baselining against your own data means every claim after launch is a comparison with your own past — the only comparison that matters.

If we cannot measure it in week one, we will not claim it in month three.

What happens next

Every month after go-live we report against that baseline: what moved, what did not, and what we are changing because of it. Targets come from your numbers, and the report says so plainly — including when something has not improved.

It is less exciting than a demo. It is also the reason a client can tell their board, with a straight face, what the system is worth.
